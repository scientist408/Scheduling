ChatGPT said:

For your use case, I would choose an HTML webpage, not a macro-enabled Excel sheet.

Why:

  • You want to click cells to mark R/OFF, then generate or rebalance the schedule interactively.

  • You want the tool to handle last-minute call-offs and replace someone from OFF without rebuilding everything.

  • You may eventually want multiple people to use it on different computers or phones.

  • A webpage is easier to make visually clean and user-friendly than VBA.

  • You already have experience with GitHub Pages, so deployment would be straightforward.

The only major limitation is that a basic GitHub Pages version does not automatically save shared data unless we add storage. But we can still make it so you can:

  • enter/update employees,

  • click requested-off days,

  • generate the schedule,

  • rebalance after call-offs,

  • copy the final schedule directly into Excel,

  • export the schedule as CSV/Excel-compatible format.

A macro Excel sheet is better only when the schedule must stay entirely inside Excel and be managed by one person on one computer.

For this project, I recommend:

HTML + JavaScript scheduling app → copy/export result to Excel

That gives you the better long-term system.

The program assigns:

B1 through B13 + 1 Rework employee every day

and fills remaining cells with either:

  • OFF

  • R/OFF

It would try to keep each employee at a maximum of 5 working days / 40 hours.

For someone who requested exactly 2 days off:

2 × R/OFF + 5 scheduled days

For someone who requested 3 days off:

3 × R/OFF + scheduled on the remaining 4 days

Step 4: Handle call-offs later

Suppose the schedule is already generated:

John — Monday B7

John calls off Monday morning.

You click John's Monday cell and change:

B7 → R/OFF

Then click:

REBALANCE / FIND REPLACEMENT

The webpage finds an employee who was OFF Monday, for example Sam:

Sam: OFF → B7

It keeps the other assignments unchanged as much as possible.

Step 5: Copy to Excel

The final result can have a button:

COPY SCHEDULE

You click it and paste directly into Excel with:

  • Employee Name

  • Employee ID

  • Monday through Sunday

  • B1–B13

  • Rework

  • OFF

  • R/OFF

We can also add Download CSV so Excel opens it directly.

One feature I strongly recommend

At the bottom of every day, show a coverage check:

Monday: ✓ B1–B13 covered | ✓ Rework covered
